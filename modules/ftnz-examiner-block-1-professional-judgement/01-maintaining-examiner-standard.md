# Maintaining the Examiner Standard

This recurrent programme assumes you already understand the examiner role, delegated authority and evidence-based assessment. The purpose is to keep experienced examiners current, reflective and aligned in how those principles are applied.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- explain why experience does not remove the need for evidence and standards;
- recognise when your assessment threshold may have moved; and
- test whether a decision is transparent and defensible to another experienced examiner.

## Experience and judgement

Experience improves pattern recognition, professional confidence and the ability to identify subtle changes in candidate performance. It can also change an examiner’s threshold. Some changes are genuine professional development; others develop through routine, local practice or increasing confidence in personal judgement.

> **Important:** Professional examiner judgement does not remain standardised simply because the examiner is experienced. It remains standardised through currency, reflection, evidence discipline and active calibration.

Professional intuition is valuable, but it is not a substitute for evidence. If an assessment decision is challenged, you must still be able to explain:

1. what you observed;
2. what the observation meant in relation to the required competency;
3. how you weighted the evidence; and
4. why that evidence supported the decision.

Experience may make the reasoning faster. It does not remove the need for it.

## The recurrent calibration question

![Four-stage pathway from evidence to a defensible examiner decision](./assets/recurrent-calibration-question.svg)

> **Calibration question:** Would another experienced examiner, presented with the same evidence and the same standard, be likely to understand and support the reasoning behind this decision?

The question does not require identical examiner personalities or techniques. It tests whether the reasoning is transparent, evidence-based and defensible.

## Key takeaways

- Experience can sharpen recognition, but conclusions must remain supported by evidence and standards.
- A defensible decision can be explained through observation, competency, evidence weighting and the applicable standard.
- Calibration is about making reasoning visible, not making every examiner identical.
