# Review and Completion

## Key points

- Assessment validity begins before the assessment itself.
- Likely competence does not correct missing prerequisites.
- Several manageable threats may collectively create an unsuitable assessment environment.
- Behaviour is observable; competence is inferred from evidence.
- Strong technical performance does not automatically demonstrate strong judgement.
- Errors are not automatically evidence of incompetence.
- Recovery provides valuable evidence but does not erase the original event.
- Examiners themselves are susceptible to bias and premature conclusions.
- Difficult decisions require separation of observation, interpretation and conclusion.

## Final check before the quiz

Ask yourself:

1. Have the assessment prerequisites and my own readiness been verified?
2. What is the cumulative effect of the current threats?
3. What exactly was observed, and what context surrounded it?
4. How quickly and independently did the candidate recognise and manage the issue?
5. What evidence would cause me to change my initial conclusion?

## Completion position

Good examiners do not simply notice errors. They determine what those errors, recoveries and behaviours demonstrate about competence against the required standard.
