# Field Application — Defensible Practice Aid

This extended application lesson turns Block 3’s principles into a repeatable communication, debrief and record-keeping routine. Use it when the assessment is difficult to explain or when a candidate challenges the outcome.

## Before the assessment — set the communication boundary

Decide what communication is needed to manage the assessment and what would contaminate the evidence. Use questions to clarify an instruction, request an explanation or resolve genuine ambiguity. Do not identify an unrecognised error, supply the desired action or repeatedly rescue a decision the candidate should make independently.

![Aviation examiner and pilot conducting a calm evidence-based post-assessment debrief](./assets/professional-debrief-scene.png)

## During the assessment — preserve the evidence

When a question is necessary, record:

1. what was observable before the question;
2. why clarification or intervention was necessary;
3. the exact neutral wording used where it matters; and
4. what the candidate did after the question.

Then consider how the question changed the evidential value of the response. A safety intervention can be necessary while still affecting how the performance should be interpreted.

## After the assessment — construct the record

Use three layers:

| Layer | Write this |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence | Observable actions, words, timings, deviations, omissions and corrections. |
| Interpretation | What the evidence may demonstrate against the competency or standard. |
| Conclusion | The outcome and why the evidence supports it. |

Include positive evidence and recovery even when the outcome is unsuccessful. Avoid labels such as “poor SA”, “overloaded” or “bad TEM” unless the observable evidence is also recorded.

## Handling challenge

Use this sequence:

1. acknowledge the reaction without conceding the decision;
2. state or restate the outcome;
3. explain the evidence and applicable standard;
4. avoid debating another examiner or operational pressure; and
5. explain any valid review or follow-up process.
