# Assessment Readiness and Legitimacy

Experienced examiners generally know what must be checked before an assessment. The recurrent issue is recognising when an apparently minor deficiency changes the question from:

> “Is the candidate competent?”

to:

> “Can a valid assessment actually take place?”

These are different questions.

## Learning outcomes

- Separate likely competence from assessment legitimacy.
- Identify the preconditions that must be verified before commencement.
- Apply the same readiness scrutiny to yourself that you apply to the candidate and aircraft.

A candidate may be highly experienced and almost certainly competent. That does not automatically mean the assessment is legitimate to conduct.

## Before commencement

Depending on the assessment, consider:

- candidate eligibility;
- prerequisite training;
- required documentation;
- examiner authority and scope;
- aircraft or simulator suitability;
- required equipment;
- operational conditions; and
- your own readiness to conduct the assessment.

> **Important:** Likely competence cannot cure an invalid assessment.

## Scenario — the missing evidence

A candidate presents for an assessment. The required training has reportedly been completed. The instructor confirms verbally that everything was done correctly. However, one required training record cannot be located. The candidate has travelled several hours to attend and the aircraft has been booked specifically for the assessment.

The primary issue is whether the required assessment prerequisites can be verified. Verbal assurance, operational inconvenience and previous performance should not replace required evidence. If the deficiency can legitimately be resolved before commencement, the assessment may then proceed. Where it cannot, likely competence does not make the assessment valid.

## Examiner readiness

Assessment readiness also applies to you. Consider fatigue, illness, workload, distraction, inadequate preparation, time pressure and recency with the assessment standard.
