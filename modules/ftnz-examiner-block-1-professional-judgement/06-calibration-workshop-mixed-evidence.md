# Calibration Workshop — Mixed Evidence Under Pressure

## Stage 1 — before commencement

A candidate presents for a proficiency assessment. The operator is short of qualified personnel. The candidate is well known and experienced. One administrative prerequisite appears incomplete, but the operator advises that the underlying training has definitely been completed and the paperwork can be corrected later.

**Decision:** What should drive the commencement decision?

Consider whether the required preconditions for a valid assessment are actually satisfied. Likely candidate competence, operational need and previous knowledge of the candidate do not replace valid prerequisites.

## Stage 2 — during the assessment

The documentation issue is resolved before commencement. The assessment begins. Early performance is strong. Later, workload increases. The candidate misses a radio call, omits one checklist item and allows navigation accuracy to deteriorate. Each event is recoverable.

**Decision:** Continue gathering evidence and assess whether the events form a meaningful workload or situational-awareness pattern. Do not fail immediately because three errors occurred, ignore them because each was recoverable, or prompt the candidate unless safety requires it.

## Stage 3 — procedural error and recovery

During a later phase, ATC instructs the candidate to change frequency. The candidate enters the frequency incorrectly and makes a call. When no response is received, the candidate initially repeats the call, then checks the radio, identifies the incorrect entry, selects the correct frequency and establishes communication without examiner assistance.

**Decision:** Assess the error, recognition and recovery as part of the complete evidence story. Determine whether it is isolated or contributes to a wider pattern against the applicable standard.

## Stage 4 — recovery and mixed evidence

After the frequency error, the candidate demonstrates stronger cross-checking and communication discipline. The remaining assessment items are performed well, with no repetition of the same error.

Recovery can demonstrate recognition, checking discipline and effective correction. It does not remove the original evidence, and it does not guarantee competence.

## Stage 5 — final judgement

The evidence is mixed. Technical performance is largely strong. There have been several minor workload-related errors, including the incorrect frequency entry, but each was recognised and recovered without examiner assistance.
