# Field Application — Evidence and Debrief Aid

This extended application lesson turns Block 1’s principles into a repeatable preparation tool. Use it before or after a difficult assessment, especially when the candidate is familiar, operational pressure is present, or the evidence is mixed.

## The five-pass review

### Pass 1 — establish legitimacy

Before interpreting performance, confirm that the assessment can be conducted validly:

- Are the required preconditions, documentation and prerequisites satisfied?
- Is the examiner authorised and ready for this assessment?
- Is the aircraft, simulator and operational environment suitable?
- Is any pressure influencing the wish to commence or continue?

If a prerequisite is unresolved, record the gap and the legitimate path to resolve it. Do not use likely competence or inconvenience to fill the gap.

### Pass 2 — preserve the observation

Write the smallest complete account of what happened. Include timing or sequence where it changes meaning, but avoid unsupported labels.

| Instead of | Record |
| --- | --- |
| “Bad judgement” | The decision made, the information available, the option chosen and the observable result. |
| “Poor situational awareness” | What was visible or communicated, what was missed, and when recognition occurred. |
| “Overloaded” | The sequence of calls, checklist actions, control changes or corrections that were observed. |

### Pass 3 — weigh the event

For each concern, ask:

1. Was it isolated or repeated?
2. What context and workload surrounded it?
3. How severe was it against the applicable standard?
4. Was it recognised independently and promptly?
5. Was the recovery appropriate and effective?
6. Did the event leave a pattern elsewhere?

Recovery is relevant evidence, but it does not erase the event that made recovery necessary.

### Pass 4 — test your judgement

Name the pressure or bias if one is present: familiarity, reputation, operator need, early-pass thinking, early-fail thinking, personal technique preference or over-weighted recovery.

> **Control question:** What evidence would cause me to change my mind?

If you cannot answer, deliberately seek disconfirming evidence where a valid opportunity remains.

### Pass 5 — explain the decision

Use this order in your debrief:

1. state the outcome;
2. identify the evidence that mattered most;
3. link it to the applicable standard or competency;
4. acknowledge significant positive evidence; and
5. explain any valid review or follow-up process.

Do not debate reputation, operational need or what another examiner might have done.
