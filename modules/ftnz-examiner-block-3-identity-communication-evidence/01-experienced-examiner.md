# The Experienced Examiner

Experience changes the examiner role. A newly authorised examiner may concentrate heavily on following the assessment sequence, remembering procedural requirements, recording enough information, applying the standard correctly and managing the mechanics of the assessment.

With experience, much of this becomes easier. That creates capacity for better observation and judgement. It can also create another risk: becoming increasingly comfortable doing things “your way”.

## Learning outcomes

- Distinguish professional examiner authority from personal authority.
- Recognise behaviours that support impartial and defensible assessment.
- Notice how your presence can influence the assessment environment.

## Confidence and authority are not the same thing

Professional examiner identity should be based on disciplined exercise of delegated authority rather than personal authority.

> “I can explain why this decision follows from the evidence and the standard.”

is stronger than:

> “I’ve been doing this long enough to know.”

The first demonstrates professional judgement. The second risks making experience itself the standard.

## Professional examiner behaviour

- preparedness;
- impartiality;
- consistency;
- calm authority;
- appropriate professional distance;
- respect for candidates;
- willingness to reconsider an initial impression;
- willingness to explain decisions;
- accurate documentation; and
- openness to calibration with other examiners.

You are part of the assessment environment. Tone, questioning style, body language and visible reactions can influence candidate performance.

## Key takeaway

Experience should improve your ability to apply and explain the required standard. It does not enlarge delegated authority or replace the need for evidence and standards.
